How it works

One address, two halves of its key.

  1. 1

    Split

    The taker holds a secret a, the maker a secret b. The taker's ZEC goes to a fresh shielded address that only a + b can spend.

  2. 2

    Lock

    The maker locks the USDC in an escrow on Base. The escrow knows the public points of a and b, never the secrets.

  3. 3

    Claim

    To take the USDC, the taker publishes a. The escrow checks it on chain with Pallas curve arithmetic written in Solidity.

  4. 4

    Settle

    Publishing a gives the maker the last piece, so the maker can spend the ZEC. If the taker never claims, the maker's refund publishes b and the taker takes the ZEC back.

No script on the Zcash side: the lock is the key split itself. Requests and quotes travel as encrypted memos inside shielded transactions.

What an observer sees

Two chains, nothing that ties them together.

A passive observer learns nothing that links the two chains beyond timing and the wallet fingerprint the transactions share. Against timing, the wallet can wait a random delay before broadcasting each step, 2.6 hours on average. The demo turns the delays off so you are not waiting hours.

Why it is safe

Nobody walks away with both sides.

An honest taker

loses at most a small premium, and is never held hostage while staying online through the claim.

An honest maker

never loses funds by coming online once in a stated window.

A commit-then-reveal claim, a Ready gate and a punish window make every exit safe, under stated bounds on inclusion delay, finality and reorganizations. The proofs are in the paper.

  • TLA+ model checking found a real attack in an earlier design: a refund that is included but not yet final could be reorganized into a theft.
  • 18 of 18 two-party scenarios pass, 121 of 121 checks, with injected reorgs, an impostor maker and malicious quotes.
  • 52 of 52 contract tests, plus Halmos proofs, fuzzing and mutation testing.
  • 3 swaps settled on Zcash testnet and Base Sepolia, every Zcash transaction fully shielded.
